Frame Screenshot → Clipboard

The exact video moment, on your clipboard, without creating a file. Hover any HTML5 video, click the camera button on the player, and the current frame is copied as a PNG at the video's native resolution — ready to paste anywhere. No editor, no downloads, no account.

Why this exists

Getting one frame out of a video usually means pausing it, screenshotting the whole screen, then cropping away the page around it — and ending up with whatever resolution your monitor happens to be. This extension collapses that to click → paste:

  1. Hover the player and click the camera button, press the page shortcut, or click the toolbar icon
  2. The decoded frame is drawn at the video's own resolution — the page's text, navigation and overlays are never part of the image
  3. Paste with Ctrl+V / Cmd+V into Slack, ChatGPT, Figma, Docs, Notion, email, tickets, image editors

Features

  • 🎬 Native-resolution frames — a 4K video gives you a 4K frame, no matter how small the player is on screen
  • 🎯 Only the video — never the hero text, the nav bar, the cookie banner, or the extension's own button
  • ⌨️ Your shortcut — a one-key or two-key page sequence, default V then S, ignored while you type
  • 🖼️ Finds every player — looped "moving photos", videos added later by single-page apps, open shadow roots, embedded frames
  • 📋 Straight to clipboard — nothing is downloaded, uploaded, or written to disk
  • 🆓 Free & open source — MIT licensed, Manifest V3, no dependencies

Install from source (developer mode)

  1. Clone the repository
  2. Open chrome://extensions and enable Developer mode
  3. Click Load unpacked and select the extension folder

FAQ

Why is the copied image sometimes smaller than the video?
A few sites hand their video to the page in a way that makes the browser refuse a direct read of the frame. The extension then reloads the media on its own or falls back to capturing the visible area, and it tells you which one it used.

Nothing happens on Netflix or Prime Video.
DRM-protected video is deliberately unreadable to extensions; the browser hands back a black frame by design.

Where is the captured frame stored?
Nowhere. The PNG goes to your clipboard and exists only there.
